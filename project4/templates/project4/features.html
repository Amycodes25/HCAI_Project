Task 1 — Feature representation

Choose a representation for a movie recommender, justify it, and write the method that extracts it from the IMDB 5000 Movie Dataset.

Why this is the whole model

Utility is defined as U(x) = wTx. That makes the feature design load-bearing in two directions at once. Anything not expressible as a linear function of x cannot be represented at all — "likes Wes Anderson films" only exists if something in x carries it. And w has to be identified from a handful of interactions, so a long vector is not estimable no matter how expressive it is. The interesting work is choosing between those.

What was left out, and why

Director and cast identity, plot keywords and country are all high-cardinality: a column per director would mean thousands of components, none of them estimable from ten interactions. The part of that signal worth keeping is carried by director prominence and popularity instead.

Gross earnings are excluded because they are largely determined by budget and popularity, which are both present. A third correlated column adds collinearity, and collinear features are exactly what makes w hard to identify from few observations.

Standardisation

Every continuous column is standardised. Utility is wTx, so a column's scale sets its weight's scale — without this the budget column, of order 108, and the genre columns, which are 0 or 1, could not share a sensible prior, and the regularisation would penalise them wildly unevenly. Vote counts, budget and director followers are logged first, since each spans several orders of magnitude.

Extraction

project4/ml/features.py reads the dataset, drops rows missing any required field, de-duplicates on title and year, and returns the design matrix. It runs once and is cached.
