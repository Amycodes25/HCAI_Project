Task 2 — Extending Bradley–Terry to rankings

Design 1 produces one pairwise comparison per trial. Design 2 produces a ranking of ten. The model has to cover both.

The standard form

Bradley–Terry gives the probability that one item is preferred to another from their utilities:

P(i ≻ j) = exp(wTxi) / [ exp(wTxi) + exp(wTxj) ]

The proposed extension

A ranking is generated by repeatedly choosing the best of what remains, each choice following the Luce rule. That gives the Plackett–Luce model:

P(i1 ≻ … ≻ in) = ∏k=1n−1 exp(wTxik) / ∑j≥k exp(wTxij)

At n = 2 this collapses exactly to Bradley–Terry, which is what makes it an extension of the model rather than a different model that happens to accept rankings.

The alternative, and why not

A ranking of ten could instead be broken into the 45 pairwise comparisons it implies and each treated as an independent Bradley–Terry observation. It is simpler, but those 45 comparisons are not independent — they come from one ordering by one person — so the likelihood is misspecified and the resulting confidence in w is overstated.

Estimating w

Regularised maximum likelihood is the floor. Lecture 9 argues for a posterior instead, and its worked example is this exact problem: recommending from "A is better than B" feedback, where a point estimate fitted on a few comparisons risks committing to preferences the data does not support. A posterior also yields an uncertainty measure that the study can use as an outcome in its own right.

Implementation

project4/ml/preference.py carries the log-likelihood, its analytic gradient, and the fit. Both interfaces feed the same estimator, since a pairwise choice is a ranking of length two — which is what makes their results comparable at all.

The log-likelihood is concave in w, and is maximised under a Gaussian prior (α = {{ alpha }}). The prior is not decoration: with roughly ten interactions and {{ n_features }} features the unpenalised maximum is not unique, because any direction the shown films do not distinguish is unconstrained and the optimiser will run off along it. The penalty holds those directions at zero, which is the honest statement that the data said nothing about them.

Two checks, both run against the implementation: at n = 2 the Plackett–Luce probability equals the Bradley–Terry probability to 10−12, and the analytic gradient agrees with central differences to 10−8.
